# E16 result: extra steps improve numerical acceptance but not target generation

E16 completed all eight cases in 3510.20 seconds/489 GGN. Numerical acceptance increased from E15's 0/8 to 1/8, but full target-token generation remained 0/8. The predeclared efficacy criterion failed. All eight cases exactly reproduced every available E15 attempt record in their initial up-to-three steps, including the two earlier terminal failures. This supports the matched step-budget comparison.

Three cases exhausted six improving steps, three failed backtracking, one failed numerical convergence and one was accepted. The accepted case3 reached batched target margin +0.125 in six steps, with reference KL 0.00002378 and drift 0.19921. Its independent native reload matched logits and both generation diagnostics exactly; its 117442152-byte matrix was deleted after the last consumer. All Base restores passed, no matrix/lease/process/failure record remained. The surviving numerical failure repeats E15's primal KKT residual 1.2398e-5 despite converged CG. The new backtracking failures preserve the original reference-KL threshold.

Crucially, case3's generated answer changed but did not equal the reference. Its lexical F1 fell from 0.11765 to 0, reducing the eight-case mean from 0.10712 to 0.09242. Both before/after normalized exact match were 0/8. These are lexical diagnostics, not a claim that every original answer was semantically wrong. In fact, the original case3 response contained the requested affirmative answer; the edited response changed its polarity. No medical efficacy claim follows from the numerical acceptance.

A read-only examination of the already-saved singleton native logits found target-content/EOS margins [0, 0.25], whereas the batched acceptance minimum was +0.125. Actual generation diverged at the first target token. This establishes an execution-path discrepancy requiring diagnosis; it does not yet isolate batch shape, sequence length, cache or generation processing as the cause. Clean reload reproducibility is not evidence that the training-style score matches generation. Further step increases or seed validation would not resolve this validity problem.
